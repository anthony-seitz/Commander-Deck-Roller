Commander Bracket Guide & FAQ

What the brackets mean, what counts as a Game Changer, and how this site builds your deck.

What are Commander Brackets?

Commander Brackets are a 1-to-5 scale for describing how powerful a Commander deck is, so a table can agree on what kind of game they want before shuffling up. Each bracket describes the experience you can expect and sets a few limits on certain cards and strategies.

BracketFeelGame ChangersMain limits
1 · ExhibitionUltra-casual. The theme and story come first, winning comes second.NoneNo mass land denial, no chained extra turns, no two-card infinite combos.
2 · CoreAbout the power of a typical preconstructed deck. Games are slow and interactive.NoneNo mass land denial, no chained extra turns, no two-card infinite combos.
3 · UpgradedStronger than a precon, with tuned synergy and better card quality.Up to 3No mass land denial, no chained extra turns, no early-game two-card infinite combos.
4 · OptimizedHigh power: fast mana, efficient interaction, strong combos.UnlimitedOnly the banned list.
5 · cEDHCompetitive. Built to win as efficiently as possible with full knowledge of the metagame.UnlimitedOnly the banned list.

The official bracket rules are updated from time to time. Check the latest Commander Brackets announcement from Wizards of the Coast for the current wording.

What is a Game Changer?

Game Changers are cards on an official list that warp games so much that they push a deck to a higher bracket. Examples include powerful tutors, fast mana, and cards that take over the game on their own. Brackets 1 and 2 allow none, Bracket 3 allows up to three, and Brackets 4 and 5 have no limit. The deck roller uses the official Game Changer list through Scryfall, so it stays current when the list changes.

How the deck roller builds a deck

1. It picks a commander

It chooses a random commander that matches your color identity, bracket, and budget. With Popular it picks from the most-played commanders, and with Deep cuts it picks from all of them.

2. It fills the 99 by role

Like a real deckbuilder, it fills slots by job: about 10 ramp, 10 card draw, 8 removal, 3 board wipes, cards that share the commander's creature type, then creatures and support spells. Lands are a mix of nonbasics and basics, with the basics split according to the deck's colored mana symbols.

3. It respects your bracket and budget

It adds exactly the number of Game Changers you choose (within the bracket limit) and leaves out extra-turn and mass land denial cards in lower brackets. It spreads your budget across the deck so the last cards still fit, choosing the cheapest printing of each card.

4. It writes a primer

Each deck comes with a short primer: the deck's goal, how it wins (with the actual finisher cards from the list), and an early/mid/late game plan.

Building from your own collection

Choose My collection under Card pool, then paste your list or upload a .txt or .csv export from Moxfield, ManaBox, Archidekt, Deckbox, or similar. The site identifies each card and builds a deck from only cards you own. If your cards can't make a legal 100-card deck under your settings, it tells you why and which commander came closest.

FAQ

Are the decks tournament-ready?

No. They're a random, playable starting point that follows deckbuilding fundamentals. Treat them as a fun challenge or a base to tune. Bracket 5 decks especially will need hand-tuning to be truly competitive.

Does it check for two-card infinite combos?

Not yet. There's no reliable public data source for every combo, so if you're playing Bracket 1–3, take a quick look before sitting down.

How accurate are prices?

Prices come from Scryfall, which uses TCGplayer market prices and updates them daily. They're estimates; actual store prices vary.

Is my collection uploaded anywhere?

Your list stays in your browser. Only the card names are sent to Scryfall so it can identify them. See the privacy policy.

Why did my collection say "No deck possible"?

A deck needs a commander plus about 60 spells in that commander's colors (with the default 36 lands). The message names the closest commander and how many cards you're missing. Try loosening your colors, raising the bracket, lowering the land count, or checking "I have basic lands."

Does it cost anything?

No. The site is free and supported by ads and affiliate links.
